SAMPLE RECOMMENDATION MEMO
==========================
This shows the FORMAT to follow, using my book data as the EXAMPLE ONLY.
Your memo must be about YOUR own company and data set.

A recommendation memo is short, and is written for the person who makes
the decision and controls the budget. They aren't technical, so it
leads with the answer, explains costs and risks in plain words, and
keeps the technical details in your evaluation.


To:       Finance director
From:     Your Name, data team
Date:     2026-03-17
Re:       Request DATA-1487, which database should we use?


RECOMMENDATION
Keep a relational database, and move it from SQLite to PostgreSQL in
the next year. Don't move to a NoSQL database now.

WHY
- Our data is inventory: books, authors, publishers, and formats. It
  fits neatly into tables, and the relationships between them matter.
  That's what relational databases are built for.
- We're a single store with about 15 staff and around 50,000 books.
  That's small for a database, so we don't need the extra scaling that
  NoSQL databases are built for.
- Our team already knows SQL. Switching to NoSQL would mean training
  or hiring.
- PostgreSQL lets several staff use the database at the same time,
  which SQLite doesn't handle well as we grow.

COST
- PostgreSQL is free and open source, so there's no license cost.
- The costs are hosting, staff time to move the data, and support.
  Free versions don't come with a help desk. Paid support is available
  from several companies if we need it. (Details and sources are in my
  evaluation.)

RISKS
- If we start the online review site we've talked about, customer
  reviews are long, unstructured text. That data might suit a document
  database better. We should look at this again if the review site
  goes ahead.
- Moving the data takes time, and needs a tested backup first.

IF WE DO NOTHING
SQLite will keep working for now, but as more staff use it at the same
time, it will get slower and errors become more likely.

NEXT STEP
Approve a small test move to PostgreSQL next quarter, using a copy of
the data, before switching for real.


TIPS
- Put the recommendation first. The reader may only read that part.
- Use the reader's language: cost, risk, time, and what happens if we
  don't do it. Save the technical words for your evaluation.
- Every claim about cost or support should be backed up in your
  evaluation, with a cited source.
- Keep it to about one page.
