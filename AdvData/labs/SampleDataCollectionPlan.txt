SAMPLE DATA COLLECTION PLAN
===========================
This shows the FORMAT to follow, using my book data as the EXAMPLE ONLY.
Your plan must be about YOUR own company and data set.

Before a company collects new data, someone has to decide what's worth
collecting, how it will get collected, and how anyone will know it's
right. Bad data collected fast is still bad data.


Company:      Independent bookstore, one shop and a website, about 15 staff
Current data: About 50,000 books, updated a few times a week. Not big data.


------------------------------------------------------------------
New data 1:     Sales from the till
What and why:   Every sale: which book, when, the price, and whether it
                was in the shop or online. This shows what actually
                sells, so we can restock the right books and stop
                ordering ones that don't sell.
How collected:  Automatically by the till system, with every sale.
                Exported to the database each night.
How much:       About 300 sales a day, so about 110,000 rows a year.
How verified:   - Each night, the total sales in the database must match
                  the till's end-of-day report.
                - Every sale must match a book in the books table. A
                  left join finds any that don't, like the data quality
                  check in the SQL Review Part 2 lab.
Permission:     No personal information is collected, so no consent is
                needed. Card numbers stay in the till system only.
Adds:           Volume and velocity

------------------------------------------------------------------
New data 2:     Website searches
What and why:   What customers search for on the website. Searches with
                no results show books people want that we don't stock.
How collected:  The website's analytics tool records every search.
How much:       About 5,000 searches a day, so nearly 2 million rows a
                year.
How verified:   - Filter out searches from bots, which can be a large
                  share of website traffic.
                - Spot check: run 10 searches ourselves and make sure
                  they show up correctly.
Permission:     Visitors must agree to analytics cookies. We only
                collect the search words, not who searched.
Adds:           Volume and velocity

------------------------------------------------------------------
New data 3:     Customer reviews
What and why:   Written reviews and star ratings from customers. These
                show which books people love, and help staff recommend
                books.
How collected:  A review form on the website, sent to customers a week
                after they buy a book.
How much:       About 20 reviews a day.
How verified:   - Only customers with a matching sale can review a book,
                  which blocks most fake reviews.
                - A staff member checks new reviews before they go live.
Permission:     Customers agree to the review terms. Only their first
                name is shown.
Adds:           Variety, since reviews are free text and don't fit
                neatly into tables


------------------------------------------------------------------
WOULD THIS MAKE IT BIG DATA?
With all three, the database would grow by about 2 million rows a year,
mostly from website searches, and add unstructured text from reviews.
That's a big jump for a small shop, but it still isn't big data by most
definitions. Volume and velocity are still small enough for one ordinary
database.

IS IT WORTH IT?
Sales data is worth it right away, because it directly affects what we
order. Searches are worth it if someone will actually look at the
results each week. Reviews are the most work to manage, so they should
wait until the first two are running well.


TIPS
- Start with the business question. "What and why" should say what
  decision the data helps someone make.
- Be specific about how much. A rough estimate with your working is
  better than "a lot".
- Every new kind of data needs a way to check it. If you can't verify
  it, say what the risk is.
- Check what you're allowed to collect. Personal data usually needs
  consent, and some kinds have laws about them.
