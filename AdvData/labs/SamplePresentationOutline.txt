SAMPLE PRESENTATION OUTLINE
===========================
This shows the FORMAT to follow, using my book data as the EXAMPLE ONLY.
Your presentation must be about YOUR own company and data.

At work, a data team's update to leadership or stakeholders is short,
starts with the bottom line, shows evidence, and ends with a clear ask.
The audience is busy and isn't technical, so every slide answers "why
should I care?"

Plan your talk slide by slide like this before you make any slides.
The times add up to about 9 minutes, which leaves room inside the
7 to 10 minute limit.


------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 1: Title (0:30)
On the slide:  Inventory Data: What We Built and What It Tells Us
               Your Name, Data Team, Independent Bookstore, April 2026
Say:           Who I am, and the bottom line in one sentence: "Our new
               inventory database shows we're overstocked on Classics
               and understocked on what customers rate highest, and
               I'm asking you to approve collecting sales data."

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 2: Our data (1:00)
On the slide:  Where the data comes from, what it covers, and the
               license, in 3 short bullets
Say:           Best Books Ever data from Zenodo, 99 books in our
               working sample, non-commercial license, so we'd need
               permission before using it to make money.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 3: What we built (1:30)
On the slide:  The final schema diagram, with the main tables labelled
Say:           We went from one messy spreadsheet to a database with
               separate tables for books, authors, and formats. Explain
               what a table is in one sentence, for anyone who doesn't
               know.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4: Live demo (1:00)
On the screen: SandboxSQL, with my database loaded
Do:            Run one query live that answers a business question,
               and read out the answer.
Say:           "This is the question the store manager asked, and
               here's the answer straight from the database."

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 5: Cleaning up the data (1:00)
On the slide:  3 problems we fixed, each with a number
               - 24 fake ISBNs marked as unknown
               - 31 missing prices left out of calculations
               - 6 digital books with no shelf section
Say:           Why each one mattered to the business.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 6: What we found (1:30)
On the slide:  My best chart from week 13, data visualization
Say:           Classics are half our catalogue, 50 of 99 books, but
               Fantasy has the highest average rating, 4.34 stars, and
               we only stock 12 Fantasy books.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 7: Looking ahead (1:00)
On the slide:  Our North Star Metric, and the dashboard mock-up
Say:           Our North Star is books sold per week. We can't measure
               it yet, because sales aren't in the database. Show what
               the dashboard would track.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 8: Recommendation and ask (1:00)
On the slide:  One sentence: Approve collecting till sales data
               starting next month.
Say:           What it costs, what we get, and what happens if we
               don't. About 300 sales a day, and it lets us track our
               North Star every week.

------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 9: Questions (0:30)
On the slide:  The one-sentence summary again, and "Questions?"
Say:           Thank them, and repeat the ask.


TIPS
- Bottom line first. If leadership only remembers one sentence, make
  sure you said it in the first 30 seconds.
- One idea per slide. If you need to read your slide out loud, it has
  too many words.
- Use real numbers from your own work, and say what each one means.
- Explain any technical word the first time you use it, or don't use it.
- Rehearse with a timer. Running over is like going over your slot in a
  real meeting: the next person loses time.
