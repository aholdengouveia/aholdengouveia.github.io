Digital Forensics

Learning outcomes:

  • Explain what computer forensics is, and where it's used
  • Identify types of digital evidence, including volatile data
  • Describe the steps of an investigation, including making a forensically sound copy
  • Describe how a CERT or CSIRT handles an incident and what happens after it

  • What is computer forensics?
    • Forensics for computers and digital storage media
    • The goal is to examine evidence in a forensically sound manner
    • Used in criminal law since the mid 1980s
    • NIST's guide to integrating forensic techniques into incident response
  • Where it's used
    • Criminal investigations
    • Civil court cases
    • Corporate IT
  • Careers in computer forensics
    • Police and law enforcement
    • Government agencies
    • Audit and compliance services
    • Corporate IT
  • Digital evidence types
    • Disk drives and secondary storage
    • Cell phones
    • Other: printers, game systems, security alarms, and IoT
    • Volatile data, which is lost when the power goes off
  • Steps of an investigation
    • Identify, collect, and preserve evidence
    • Evidence analysis
    • Reporting and testimony
  • Duplication and authentication
    • A forensically correct copy: a drive image, bit stream image, or ISO image
    • Write blockers, and special software and hardware for duplication
    • Forensically wiped drives
    • Hashes to prove the copy matches the original
  • CERT and CSIRT teams
    • Dealing with an incident
    • Team members, including stakeholders and the C-suite
    • Prioritizing: shut it all down, or work around it?
  • After an incident
    • Identify and preserve evidence: forensic copies, logs, and histories
    • Analysis: what happened, why, damage control, and data loss
    • Reports and documentation
    • Prevention: changes to structure, policy, or personnel
    • Example: after its July 2026 breach, Hugging Face published a post-mortem covering what happened, when, what was accessed, and what it changed (Cloud Security Alliance)

Suggested Activities and Discussion Topics:

  • Discussion: what devices in your home could hold digital evidence?
  • Discussion: why does a forensic investigator work from a copy instead of the original drive?
  • Activity: look up a court case that used digital evidence. What kind of evidence was it, and how was it collected?
