\section*{How to Pick a Topic}
Pick one topic from this list for your final presentation. The full instructions are in the Final Presentation document, linked under Resources.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Topics are first come, first served. Claim yours by starting a thread in the Final Presentation discussion on Brightspace, as the instructions explain.
    \item Two students can't present the same topic. If someone has already claimed yours, pick another one.
    \item You may pick a topic that isn't on this list, but check with me first.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Topics}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Passkeys:} how passkeys work, why they're replacing passwords, and how to set one up
    \item \textbf{Prompt injection and AI chatbot security:} how attackers trick AI chatbots and assistants into ignoring their rules or leaking data
    \item \textbf{Attacks on AI models:} data poisoning, model theft, and fake AI models, and how companies protect their AI
    \item \textbf{SIM swapping:} how attackers take over a phone number, and how to protect yours
    \item \textbf{Scams aimed at college students:} fake jobs, fake rentals, ticket resale scams, and how to spot them
    \item \textbf{Gaming account security:} account theft, malware hidden in cheats and mods, and in-game trading scams
    \item \textbf{Payment app scams:} how scammers use Venmo, Zelle, and Cash App, and how to protect your money
    \item \textbf{Cryptocurrency scams and wallet security:} common crypto scams, and how people protect their wallets
    \item \textbf{Romance scams and dating app safety:} how romance scams work, and how to stay safe on dating apps
    \item \textbf{Ransomware as a business:} how ransomware gangs run like companies, with affiliates, help desks, and negotiators
    \item \textbf{Election security:} how voting machines, voter rolls, and election results are protected
    \item \textbf{Attacks on critical infrastructure:} attacks on water systems, power grids, and pipelines, and how they're defended
    \item \textbf{Hospital cybersecurity:} why hospitals are targeted, and what happens to patient care during an attack
    \item \textbf{Medical device security:} risks to pacemakers, insulin pumps, and other connected medical devices
    \item \textbf{Car security:} keyless entry relay attacks, connected car apps, and car hacking research
    \item \textbf{Drone security and privacy:} how drones can be hacked or misused, and the rules around them
    \item \textbf{Satellite and space security:} why satellites and GPS are targets, and what happens when they're attacked
    \item \textbf{Quantum computing and encryption:} why quantum computers could break today's encryption, and what post-quantum encryption is
    \item \textbf{Bug bounty programs:} how companies pay hackers to find bugs, and how people get started
    \item \textbf{Careers in cybersecurity:} the main job roles, certifications, and how to get started in the field
    \item \textbf{Red, blue, and purple teams:} how attackers and defenders practice against each other inside companies
    \item \textbf{Zero trust security:} what ``never trust, always verify'' means, and how companies use it
    \item \textbf{Software supply chain attacks:} attacks like SolarWinds and the XZ Utils backdoor, where trusted software is used to spread an attack
    \item \textbf{Open-source software security:} who looks after the free code everyone relies on, and what happens when it has a bug
    \item \textbf{Commercial spyware:} spyware like Pegasus that's sold to governments, who it's used against, and how it's found
    \item \textbf{OSINT (open source intelligence):} what can be found about a person or company from public information, and how to limit it
    \item \textbf{Data brokers:} how companies collect and sell personal information, and how to opt out
    \item \textbf{Biometrics:} how face and fingerprint unlock work, how secure they are, and what happens if they're stolen
    \item \textbf{Wearables and health data:} what fitness trackers and smartwatches collect, and who can see it
    \item \textbf{Public Wi-Fi and evil twin hotspots:} how fake Wi-Fi networks trick people, and how to stay safe on public Wi-Fi
    \item \textbf{Email authentication:} how SPF, DKIM, and DMARC help stop spoofed and phishing emails
    \item \textbf{Disinformation and bot networks:} how fake accounts spread false information, and how platforms try to find them
    \item \textbf{Digital IDs:} mobile driver's licenses and digital IDs, and how they're kept secure
    \item \textbf{Cyber insurance:} what it covers, why companies buy it, and how it's changing security
    \item \textbf{Security for programmers:} common coding mistakes that lead to security bugs, and how developers avoid them
\end{enumerate}
