How a firewall decides

A firewall checks each piece of traffic against its list of rules, from the top down. The first rule that matches wins, and the firewall stops looking. If no rule matches, the default policy decides: deny all blocks it, and allow all lets it through.

Each rule says what to do (allow or deny), and what traffic it applies to: the protocol (TCP, UDP, or ICMP, which is used by ping), the port (which service, such as 443 for secure websites or 22 for SSH), and the source (who the traffic comes from).

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere. Your rules are saved in this browser, so you can come back to them.
