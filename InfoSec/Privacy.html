Privacy, Anonymity, and Paranoia

Learning outcomes:

  • Identify what your browser, smartphone, search engine, and social media know about you
  • Explain how tracking methods such as cookies, browser fingerprinting, and app permissions work
  • Evaluate your own privacy settings, and choose changes that protect your information
  • Create a threat model for a situation, and prioritize the risks

  • What does the AI in everything know?
    • Meta: since December 16, 2025, chats with Meta AI are used to target ads, with no opt-out in the US (TechCrunch)
    • LinkedIn: uses member data to train AI by default, so you have to opt out (TechCrunch)
    • Google: a 2026 settings change keeps more of your uploaded images, audio, and video to improve its AI (TechCrunch)
    • Microsoft Recall: screenshots your screen every few seconds. After privacy experts raised alarms, Microsoft made it opt-in, and apps like Signal now block it (CNN)
    • Hidden or confusing opt-outs are a dark pattern, a design meant to push you into a choice

Suggested Activities and Discussion Topics:

  • Activity: visit webkay and look at what your browser shares. What surprised you?
  • Activity: check the app permissions on your phone, and turn off at least one you don't need.
  • Discussion: how much privacy are you willing to give up for convenience?
  • Activity: find the AI training setting on one account you use, such as LinkedIn or Google, and turn it off. Was it easy to find?
