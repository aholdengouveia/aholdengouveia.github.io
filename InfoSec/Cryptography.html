Cryptography

Learning outcomes:

  • Explain the difference between symmetric and asymmetric encryption
  • Describe how public and private keys, hashes, and digital signatures are used
  • Encrypt stored data using a tool such as VeraCrypt
  • Describe legal issues around encryption, such as backdoors and key disclosure laws

  • What is encryption?
    • Encryption and decryption
    • Who cares? Anyone who stores or sends private information
  • Symmetric and asymmetric encryption
    • Symmetric: one shared key
    • Asymmetric: a public key and a private key
  • Computer encryption history
    • Enigma machines
    • DES (Data Encryption Standard)
      • Released 1977
      • Triple DES
      • Publicly broken in 1999
    • AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)
      • Published 2001
      • Approved for NSA classified data in 2003
  • Cryptanalysis
    • How much information is known: ciphertext only, known plaintext, or chosen plaintext
    • Frequency analysis
  • The love story of Alice and Bob
    • Public and private keys: what happens if the private key is lost?
    • Digital signatures
    • One way functions and hash functions
    • xkcd: Alice and Bob
  • Encrypting stored data
    • Mobile data
    • Cloud data
    • Backups
    • Password control and management
  • Encrypting communications
    • Email
    • Chat
    • Timed encryptions
  • Blockchain
    • A continuously growing list of records
    • Blocks linked and secured using cryptography
    • An example of distributed computing, such as Bitcoin
  • Legal issues
    • Backdoors and golden keys
    • The US used to treat encryption as a weapon for export, under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations
    • Key disclosure laws, such as In re Boucher

Suggested Activities and Discussion Topics:

  • In class activity: use VeraCrypt to create a 20 MB encrypted container, mount it, add some files, and dismount it. What does the container look like in File Explorer when it's dismounted?
  • Discussion: should governments have a backdoor into encryption? Who benefits, and who is put at risk?
  • Hands-on practice: try the free TryHackMe rooms Cryptography for Dummies and Crack the Hash. They run in safe practice environments built for learning, with a free account.
  • Hands-on practice: try the Cryptography and General Skills challenges in picoCTF, a free practice platform from Carnegie Mellon University, now part of the CyLab Security Academy.
