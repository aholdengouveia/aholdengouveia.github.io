Example Recovery Checklists

Industry Example: Infected Workstation

This is a starting point for a company checklist. A real one should match the company's own incident response plan, tools and contacts.

1. Report and identify

  • Record who reported the problem, when, and what they saw
  • Assign one person to lead the incident
  • Open an incident ticket and start a timeline

2. Contain

  • Disconnect the computer from the network (unplug the cable or turn off Wi-Fi) but leave it powered on
  • Disable the user's account or reset their password from a clean computer
  • Block any known bad IP addresses or domains at the firewall
  • Check whether other computers show the same signs

3. Preserve evidence

  • Photograph the screen, including any ransom note or error message
  • Capture a memory image if you have the tools and training
  • Export the logs (firewall, antivirus or EDR, sign-in logs)
  • Record chain of custody for any drive that is removed

4. Notify

  • Management, following the incident response plan
  • Legal or compliance staff if sensitive data may be involved
  • The cyber insurance provider, before hiring outside help, since many policies require this
  • Law enforcement, if needed

5. Remove and rebuild

  • Decide whether to clean or rebuild (rebuild if in doubt)
  • Wipe the computer and reinstall from a current known good image
  • Install all patches before reconnecting it to the network
  • Restore data, not programs, from a backup taken before the infection, and scan it first

6. Verify and return to use

  • Scan with at least two different tools
  • Monitor the computer's network traffic for 48 to 72 hours
  • Have the user sign in with a new password and MFA
  • Confirm with the user that everything they need works

7. Close out

  • Finish the incident report and timeline
  • Hold a blameless lessons learned meeting within two weeks
  • Update this checklist and any training

Personal Example: Your Own Computer or Phone

  1. Disconnect from Wi-Fi and unplug any network cable
  2. Don't pay anything, and don't call any phone number shown in a pop-up
  3. From a different device you trust, change your email password first, then banking, then everything else. Turn on MFA as you go
  4. Check your email settings for forwarding rules or a changed recovery phone number or email address
  5. Check your bank and card statements. If anything looks wrong, call the number on the back of your card
  6. Run a full scan with your antivirus, such as Windows Security or the free version of Malwarebytes
  7. If problems continue, copy your personal files (documents and photos only) to a backup and factory reset the device
  8. Restore your files and reinstall apps from official app stores only
  9. Tell friends and family if your accounts sent strange messages
  10. Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and if your identity was stolen, go to IdentityTheft.gov
