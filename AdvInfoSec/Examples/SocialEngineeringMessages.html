Example Messages to Review

All companies, people, phone numbers and links in these examples are made up. Some are scams and some are legitimate. Decide for yourself before opening the red flags.

Email 1

From: Northfield Bank Security <alerts@northfie1d-bank.com>
Subject: URGENT: Unusual sign-in activity, account will be suspended

Dear Customer,

We detected an unusual sign-in to your online banking account from a new device. For your protection, your account will be suspended in 24 hours unless you verify your identity.

[Verify My Account] (the button links to https://northfie1d-bank.com.verify-login.net/secure)

Thank you,
Northfield Bank Security Team

Red flags for Email 1
  • Scam
  • The sender's domain uses the number 1 in place of the letter l ("northfie1d")
  • The link actually goes to verify-login.net. The real domain is the last part before the first single slash
  • "Dear Customer" instead of your name
  • A 24 hour deadline and a threat to suspend the account
  • What to do: don't click. Log in by typing the bank's address yourself, or call the number on the back of your card

Email 2

From: Dana Whitfield <dana.whitfield.ceo@gmail.com>
Subject: Quick favor

Are you at your desk? I'm in meetings all day and need you to pick up six $200 gift cards for client thank-yous. Please keep this between us for now, it's a surprise.

Send me photos of the card numbers as soon as you have them. I'll make sure you're paid back today.

Dana
Sent from my iPhone

Red flags for Email 2
  • Scam (this is called business email compromise, or CEO fraud)
  • The CEO is using a personal Gmail account instead of the company email
  • Gift cards are almost never a legitimate business payment
  • Asking you to keep it secret stops you from checking with anyone
  • Uses authority and urgency ("in meetings all day")
  • What to do: contact Dana through a known channel, like their office phone or a company chat, and report the email

Email 3

From: IT Help Desk <helpdesk@lakesidecollege.edu>
Subject: Scheduled maintenance Saturday 6 AM to 10 AM

Hello,

Email and the student portal will be unavailable this Saturday from 6 AM to 10 AM while we install updates. You don't need to do anything.

If you have questions, call the Help Desk at extension 4357 or visit us in the library. Remember, IT will never ask for your password.

Lakeside College IT

Red flags for Email 3
  • Most likely legitimate
  • It comes from the college's real domain, and it doesn't ask you to click anything, log in, or send information
  • It gives you ways to check that you already know about (a phone extension and a physical location)
  • Even so, if you weren't sure, you could call the Help Desk to confirm

Email 4

From: Accounts Payable <billing@supp1ier-invoices.net>
Subject: Overdue invoice #48213, final notice
Attachment: Invoice_48213.pdf.zip

Hello,

Our records show invoice #48213 is now 30 days overdue. Please review the attached invoice and arrange payment today to avoid late fees and collection.

If the invoice doesn't display correctly, click "Enable Content" after opening it.

Accounts Payable

Red flags for Email 4
  • Scam (this is a common way to deliver malware)
  • The attachment ends in .zip, not .pdf. A double extension hides the real file type
  • "Enable Content" turns on macros, which can run malicious code
  • No company name, no contact person, and a generic sender address
  • Pressure: "final notice", late fees, collection
  • What to do: don't open the attachment. Check with your accounts payable team whether this vendor and invoice exist

Text Message 1

ParcelPoint: Your package could not be delivered due to an incomplete address. Update within 12 hrs to avoid return: parcelpoint-redelivery.top/xk2

Red flags for Text 1
  • Scam
  • Delivery companies don't text you links to fix an address with a deadline
  • The odd domain ending (.top) and the short random link
  • It works because most people are expecting a package at any given time
  • What to do: if you are expecting a package, check the tracking number on the store's website or app

Text Message 2

Hi Mom, I dropped my phone in the sink, this is my new number. Can you send $300 for a replacement? I'll pay you back. Don't call, the mic on this phone is broken

Red flags for Text 2
  • Scam (sometimes called the "Hi Mom" scam)
  • A new unknown number, an urgent request for money, and a reason you can't call to check
  • No name is used, so the scammer can send the same text to thousands of people
  • What to do: call or text your child on their old number, or use a family code word

Text Message 3

Lakeside College: Your verification code is 482913. Do not share this code with anyone. We will never call or text to ask for it.

Red flags for Text 3
  • Legitimate, if you just tried to log in
  • If you didn't just try to log in, someone else may have your password. Change it right away
  • A common follow-up scam is a call or text saying "we sent you a code by mistake, please read it back to us". Never share the code

Text Message 4

Toll Services: You have an unpaid toll balance of $4.35. Pay now to avoid a $50 late fee: tollpay-services.info

Red flags for Text 4
  • Scam
  • A small amount makes people pay without thinking, and the goal is to steal your card details
  • Threat of a much bigger late fee
  • No toll agency name and a generic domain
  • What to do: if you think you owe a toll, go to your toll agency's website by typing the address yourself

Text Message 5

Northfield Bank Fraud Alert: Did you attempt a purchase of $1,249.99 at ElectroMart? Reply YES or NO. To speak to an agent call 1-888-555-0199

Red flags for Text 5
  • Could be either. Real banks send texts like this, and scammers copy them
  • The risk is the phone number. If you call it, a "fraud agent" may ask for your card number, PIN or a code to "cancel" the charge
  • What to do: don't use the number in the text. Call the number on the back of your card or check your account in the bank's app
